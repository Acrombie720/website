How this index works

We stuck to five rules.

  • Only published figures. Every number came from a named source you can go and read yourself. We have not modelled or estimated anything.
  • Sample size and date on all of them. 74% of a thousand hiring managers surveyed last June means something quite different from 74% of a sample nobody will describe. You should be able to tell which one you are reading.
  • Secondhand numbers are marked. Now and then we could not get hold of the original report. Where that happened, the entry says who we took it from.
  • Nothing gets adjusted. We keep whatever rounding and wording the original used. If a publisher broke a number into parts, you get the parts.
  • Tell us when we get it wrong. If we have misread your data, or you have published something newer, send it over and it gets fixed next month.

A handful are marked sample not disclosed. Those are here because people quote them constantly, not because we rate them alongside the properly surveyed ones. Worth bearing in mind when you use them.

Methodology

Sources
  • Nine named publications: TestGorilla, ZipRecruiter Economic Research, iCIMS Insights, Study.com, ManpowerGroup, Anthropic, CompTIA, Ashby, and IDC and Lightcast via secondary reporting.
  • Every figure links back to wherever it came from. The full list sits in the Sources section above.
Sample size
49 figures across 9 sources. Underlying samples range from 1,000 respondents to 39,063 employers across 41 countries; three sources do not disclose a sample.
Date range
Fieldwork from July 2025 to September 2026. Compiled 14 September 2026.
Limitations
  • This is a compilation, not original research. We gathered and labelled these figures. We did not produce a single one of them.
  • Figures marked sample not disclosed are here because they get quoted constantly, not because they stand up as well as the properly surveyed ones.
  • Two entries come from secondhand reporting, because we could not get at the original. Both name the publication we took them from.
  • Every source means something slightly different by “AI fluency”. You cannot really compare figures across publishers, so we never add them up or average them.
  • Nearly all of it is people describing their own behaviour in surveys. Almost nothing here watches what anyone actually does.
