FluencyFox Privacy Policy

Effective date: 01/09/2026  ·  Last updated: 01/09/2026

The item still marked below needs a decision from Calyptus before this goes live.

FluencyFox is an AI-powered assessment platform provided by Calyptus Limited ("Calyptus", "we", "us"), a company registered in England and Wales (company number 13822106), registered office 71–75 Shelton Street, Covent Garden, London WC2H 9JQ.

This policy explains how we handle personal data when you visit our website, and when you take a FluencyFox assessment. We wear two different hats depending on who you are: read the section that applies to you.

1. Who this policy applies to

  • Website visitors and prospective customers: if you browse fluencyfox.com, fill in a contact form, or talk to our sales team, Calyptus is the data controller for that information. See Section 2.
  • Candidates who take a FluencyFox assessment: if you've been invited by an employer or recruiter to record a FluencyFox assessment, Calyptus is the data processor, acting on that organisation's instructions. The organisation that invited you is the data controller and is responsible for your data rights request in the first instance. See Section 3.

2. If you're a website visitor or prospective customer

What we collect: name, email, company, job title, and anything you submit through a contact or demo-request form; usage and analytics data from cookies (see Section 8).

Why: to respond to enquiries, run our sales process, and understand how our website is used.

Legal basis: our legitimate interest in running a business, and/or your consent where required (e.g. for non-essential cookies).

Retention: for as long as we have an active relationship or ongoing conversation, plus a reasonable period after (typically 6 months) unless you ask us to delete it sooner.

3. If you're a candidate taking a FluencyFox assessment

What we collect

  • Name and email address
  • Video and audio recording of you completing the assessment
  • Screen recording of your activity during the assessment
  • A transcript of your spoken narration
  • Your assessment score and feedback

We do not use facial recognition or other biometric identification, and FluencyFox is not designed to capture special category data (e.g. health, race, religion). Because you speak freely during the assessment, you might incidentally mention something sensitive; we don't extract, tag, or use any such incidental disclosure as a scoring input, and it's deleted on the same timeline as the rest of your data.

Why we process it, and on whose behalf

We process your data to deliver the assessment and produce a score, on the instructions of the organisation that invited you ("the Client"). The Client decides who takes the assessment and what happens with the result; they are the controller. Questions about why you were asked to take the assessment, what the result will be used for, or requests to access/delete your data should go to the Client first. We'll assist them with your request, and we're also directly reachable at info@calyptus.co.

Automated scoring

FluencyFox uses AI (currently OpenAI) to help transcribe and score your recorded response, prompted against an evaluation framework the inviting Client defines for its assessment. Your score is generated across weighted dimensions covering your submitted work, any interaction with an AI helper tool during the task, and your spoken communication, combined into a single overall score.

Your score is advisory only. The Client's hiring decision is made by a human reviewer; FluencyFox does not make or automatically enforce a hiring decision on the Client's behalf.

No candidate identity or demographic information (your name, photograph, video, or voice audio) is provided to the scoring model; scoring works from your assessment transcript and submitted work product only. The scoring rubric treats transcription mishearings or ambiguity caused by accent, dialect, or background noise as not attributable to you, and your narration is judged on content and coherence rather than fluency, talk-time, or transcription polish.

On request, the Client can obtain a factor-level breakdown of how a score was generated from Calyptus (which dimensions drove the result and how they were weighted). We can't provide a full trace of the underlying AI model's internal reasoning; that isn't something we or our AI provider can practically produce.

Calyptus tests its scoring rubric for adverse impact at least annually, covering sex, ethnicity, age, disability, and accent/regional speech pattern (as a potential proxy for race or national origin), using anonymised hiring-outcome data the Client provides. This testing is currently a joint exercise between Calyptus and the Client, not an independent third-party audit. Where a jurisdiction you're evaluated in requires an independent bias audit and public disclosure (for example, New York City's Local Law 144), the Client is responsible for arranging that and for giving you any required advance notice; Calyptus supports that process on request. [Update this paragraph once Calyptus has its own independent audit process in place.]

Who we share it with

  • OpenAI: for transcription and AI-assisted scoring, via API, using OpenAI's EU data residency setting. OpenAI is contractually prohibited from using your data to train or improve its models.
  • The Client who invited you: they receive your recording, transcript, and score.
  • Infrastructure providers who host the platform (see Section 6).

We do not sell your data, and we do not use it to train any AI model, ours or anyone else's.

We may also derive anonymised, aggregated analytics from use of the platform (for example, usage patterns or score distributions) that is not personal data. This belongs to Calyptus and may be used to operate, support, and improve the Service.

Where it's processed

Our application and database are hosted in the UK/EU. Recordings are stored in a private, access-controlled bucket in Amsterdam (EU). Any transfer outside the UK/EU is made under the UK International Data Transfer Agreement or EU Standard Contractual Clauses.

How long we keep it

Your data is retained for the duration of our agreement with the Client, and deleted or returned within 60 days of that agreement ending, unless the Client or we are required by law to keep it longer. Raw recording data is deleted immediately after successful submission; abandoned or incomplete recordings are cleaned up within about an hour.

Your rights

Subject to applicable law, you can ask to access, correct, delete, or receive a copy of your data, or object to or restrict its processing. Because the Client is the controller, we'll typically direct your request to them and assist them in responding, usually within 5 business days of being notified. You can also contact us directly at info@calyptus.co.

If you're in the UK, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office (ico.org.uk). If you're in the EU, you can complain to your local data protection authority. If you're a California resident, you have rights under the CCPA/CPRA, including the right to know, delete, and opt out of sale/sharing; we do not sell or share your data as defined under that law.

4. How we secure your data

  • TLS encryption for all data in transit
  • Recordings stored in a private bucket, accessible only via time-limited, presigned URLs
  • Tokenised, unguessable assessment links: no separate candidate account or password to be compromised
  • Admin access restricted and authenticated via Google sign-in with signed session cookies
  • API keys and credentials encrypted at rest (AES-256-GCM)

5. Sub-processors

We maintain an up-to-date list of sub-processors and will provide it on request. Current sub-processors include OpenAI (transcription and scoring) and our infrastructure provider (application, database, and recordings hosting, EU/UK region). We give at least 30 days' notice before adding a new sub-processor.

6. International data transfers

Where personal data is transferred outside the UK or EU/EEA, we rely on the UK IDTA, the UK Addendum, or the EU Standard Contractual Clauses, as applicable.

7. Data Processing Agreements

If you're a business customer, our standard Data Processing Agreement (incorporated into our Master Services Agreement) governs how we process Candidate Data on your behalf. Available on request.

8. Cookies

We use cookies and similar tracking technologies to operate our website and improve your experience. Cookies fall into the following categories:

  • Essential cookies – required for the website to function (e.g. security, load balancing, remembering your cookie preferences). These cannot be switched off.
  • Analytics cookies – help us understand how visitors use our site (e.g. pages visited, time on site) so we can improve it. Used with your consent.
  • Marketing cookies – used to deliver relevant ads and measure campaign performance across our site and other platforms. Used with your consent.

When you first visit our website, a cookie banner lets you accept all, reject non-essential, or customise your preferences by category. You can change your choices at any time via the "Cookie Settings" link in the site footer, or by adjusting your browser settings to block or delete cookies (note this may affect site functionality).

We do not use cookies to collect sensitive personal information.

9. Changes to this policy

We may update this policy from time to time. Material changes will be reflected by an updated "last updated" date above, and where required, we'll notify affected Clients or candidates.
